Chroma Encyclopedia

An annotated encyclopedia and bibliography of color in film: the psychology of each hue, the dimensions and contrasts of color, palette structures, narrative devices, auteur signatures, the processes and stocks behind historical looks, the photography of skin, and the literature behind all of it.

Index

    How to read this. Every film appears as a frame tile showing an AI re-enactment: the scene's lighting, palette and composition re-staged in a Nigerian setting with original characters (never the film's actors, characters or costumes). Each tile is tagged so it is never mistaken for a real still, and every example carries a short Readings note on its implications and alternative interpretations. Each tile links to that film's real stills (FILMGRAB and an image search; ShotDeck needs a login, so search it by title there). To embed real frames, put your own grabs in a stills/ folder next to this file, named as in the manifest (§9), e.g. stills/moonlight.jpg. They load automatically and click to enlarge; any missing still falls back to the palette. Every hue, palette, narrative device and auteur also opens with an illustrative frame: an original image generated with Google Flow and set in Nigerian contexts. These frames are labelled AI-generated and are never presented as stills from the cited films. They live in frames/; drop a catalog render with the same filename in to replace any of them. Hex palettes are indicative approximations, not values sampled from frames. Chips like C5 or L6 point to the matching look or lighting ID in the Film Visual Styles Catalog v2. Click any swatch to copy its hex value. Meanings are cultural conventions; context always decides.

    1. Color Psychology: the Hues

    What each hue tends to do to an audience, on both its bright side and its dark side, how cinematographers get it into the frame, and the films that defined it.

    2. Dimensions & Contrasts

    Hue is only one of the controls. Saturation, value and temperature often carry more of the emotion, and Johannes Itten's seven contrasts give the frame its tension.

    Itten's Seven Color Contrasts, applied to film

    Extension Study: shot size and angle change color

    The same red gele, the same woman, the same neutral Enugu market. Only the framing changes. Shot size changes how much of the frame red covers (Itten's contrast of extension); angle changes what that red means.

    Recurring cast

    The milieu contrast sets reuse six described characters so differences read as milieu and color, not random casting. They are AI-generated people, not real persons.

    3. Palette Structures

    Color-wheel harmonies that set the structure of a frame. In practice one color dominates, a second supports, and a third is used sparingly as an accent (the 60-30-10 rule of thumb).

    4. Narrative Color Devices

    Ways color tells the story without dialogue: it orients the viewer in place and time, assigns themes to characters, foreshadows, and maps a story's arc.

    5. Auteur & Cinematographer Signatures

    Color languages recognizable across whole filmographies. Each entry credits the cinematographer, who usually built the look with the director.

    6. Processes, Stocks & Historical Looks

    Most named looks come from a specific technology: a dye, a lab process, a stock, or a pipeline. Knowing the process is how you rebuild the look honestly.

    Color science was not neutral. Its reference standards were built around light skin, and an encyclopedia of film color has to cover that history and the practice that answers it.
